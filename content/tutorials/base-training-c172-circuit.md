Every pilot learns to land by flying the same rectangle around a runway, over and over. The circuit gives each landing an identical setup: same height, same distance, same speed at every turn. When the setup is the same, you can see what went wrong on the last one and fix it on the next.

This lesson flies a standard left-hand circuit at 1,000 ft above the airfield in the default Cessna 172. Pick a quiet airfield with a long runway and set the weather to calm. Fly six circuits before you judge yourself.

![The left-hand circuit and the settings on each leg: upwind full power, rotate at 55 kt, climb at 75-85 kt; crosswind turn at about 500 ft and level at 1,000 ft; downwind 1,000 ft, 2,200 RPM, 90 kt; abeam the threshold 1,500 RPM, flaps 10, 80 kt; base flaps 20, 70 kt, 500 fpm; final flaps full, 65 kt](/images/news/base-training-c172-circuit/c172-circuit-cyan.webp)

## Upwind

Full power, rotate at 55 knots, and climb at 75 to 85 knots. Hold the runway centreline with the rudder. At 500 ft, check behind you that the runway is where you left it.

:::checklist Upwind
Throttle | FULL
Rotate | 55 KIAS
Climb Speed | 75-85 KIAS
Flaps | UP | If you took off with 10°, retract above 300 ft
:::

## Crosswind

Turn 90° left at around 500 ft and keep climbing. Level off at 1,000 ft above the airfield, then let the speed build before you reduce power.

## Downwind

Fly parallel to the runway, about a mile out. The runway should sit roughly halfway up the left wing strut. Do the downwind checks here; this is the calmest part of the circuit.

:::checklist Downwind
Altitude | 1,000 FT AGL
Power | 2,200 RPM | About 90 knots level
Fuel Selector | BOTH
Mixture | FULL RICH
Landing Lights | ON
:::

Abeam the threshold, bring the power back to about 1,500 RPM and hold the nose up as the speed bleeds off. Below 110 knots, set the first stage of flap.

:::checklist Abeam Threshold
Power | 1,500 RPM
Flaps 10 | BELOW 110 KIAS
Speed | 80 KIAS
:::

## Base

When the threshold is 45° behind your left shoulder, turn base. You should be descending at around 500 feet per minute. Judge the height here. Too high, add flap early. Too low, add power now rather than on final.

:::checklist Base
Flaps 20 | BELOW 85 KIAS
Speed | 70 KIAS
Descent Rate | 500 FPM
:::

## Final

Roll out on the centreline with the runway numbers fixed in the windscreen. If they move up, you're sinking too fast. If they move down, you're too high. Control speed with pitch and the descent path with power.

:::checklist Final
Flaps | FULL
Speed | 65 KIAS
Runway | CLEAR
:::

Over the threshold, close the throttle and raise the nose to about 5° as the ground comes up. Hold it off until the main wheels touch.

## Touch-and-go

Keep straight with the rudder, flaps up, full power, and you're back on upwind. If the approach didn't feel right at any point, go around: full power, flaps to 20°, climb at 60 knots, and clean up once climbing.

:::checklist Go-Around
Throttle | FULL
Flaps | 20 DEG
Climb | 60 KIAS
Flaps | UP | In stages once the climb is established
:::

A good circuit takes about five minutes. When six in a row land within the first third of the runway at 65 knots, move on to crosswind circuits. The [Cessna 172 checklist](/aircraft/c172) covers the full flight from startup to shutdown.
